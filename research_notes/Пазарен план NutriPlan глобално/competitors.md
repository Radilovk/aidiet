# Competitive landscape: AI / personalized nutrition and meal-planning apps (global, English-speaking markets), as of Nov 2026

Note on sources: many 2026 "comparison" pages come from competing apps (Nutrola, Fitia, Welling, Nutriscan, Unstar, Rekkon). Treat their feature claims and rankings as vendor-biased. Primary sources are used where available: company press releases, earnings transcripts, App Store listings, and TechCrunch, Fierce Healthcare and MobiHealthNews. Research ran about 17 tool calls, so coverage is broad but not exhaustive.

## 1. Main players by segment and their 2025–2026 positioning

### Takeaway
The market has split three ways:
- **Mass-market trackers** are consolidating around AI photo logging. MyFitnessPal bought Intent (meal planning, 2025) and Cal AI (photo logging, March 2026).
- **Legacy behavior-change brands** have pivoted hard to GLP-1 prescribing. WW did this after its May 2025 Chapter 11; Noom did it with microdose GLP-1 and "Proactive Health".
- **Insurance-billed dietitian platforms** are raising large rounds and adding AI. Fay raised $50M at a $500M valuation; Nourish raised $100M Series C in May 2026.

Condition-specific players are mostly employer- or payer-funded clinics (Virta, Twin, Midi), not consumer apps.

### Inferences
- **Mass market:** "AI photo logging" became table stakes in 2025–26, since MFP bought rather than built it. It is no longer a differentiator; menu photo analysis that recommends a dish is likely still less common.
- **GLP-1 pivot:** WW and Noom now compete mainly as telehealth GLP-1 prescribers, at roughly $79–$199+/mo for medication programs. That leaves the non-drug, food-first, structured-plan user less served by these brands.
- **Clinical platforms:** dietitian-backed services that scale (Fay, Nourish) are US insurance-billing businesses. They are not available as cheap self-pay apps in the UK, CA or AU, and they are not built around a ready-made exact-gram weekly menu.
- **Condition programs:** these are mostly B2B or payer channels (Virta, Twin, Midi), not something a bootstrapped consumer app competes with directly. Consumer apps covering many protocols in one place appear to be rare.

### Gaps

- IBS apps (Nerva, Cara, Monash FODMAP) and PCOS-specific apps were not researched.

## 2. Who combines exact-gram meal plans + adaptive weekly adjustment + human expert review + AI chat?

### Takeaway
No competitor found in this research publicly combines all four in one consumer app. The pieces are split across segments:
- Exact-quantity plan generators (Eat This Much, PlateJoy, Mealime, Intent/MFP) have no human review.
- Adaptive calorie algorithms are tracking-based (MacroFactor-style) and come with no menu.
- Human dietitian review is sold as insurance-billed sessions (Fay, Nourish) or high-priced coaching (Noom, WW).
- AI chat is being bolted onto trackers and clinics (Nourish's RD AI assistant, MFP's ChatGPT Health integration).

### Cited Findings
- MFP/Intent produces tailored meal plans plus grocery lists using machine learning. Nothing indicates dietitian sign-off — [CO/AI](https://getcoai.com/news/myfitnesspal-acquires-intent-to-combine-meal-planning-grocery-list-creation-and-calorie-tracking-in-one-ai-app/)
- Nourish's AI assistant supports RDs rather than users directly. Meal planning was still listed as "coming in the next months" — [Pulse](https://www.pulse.bot/entrepreneurship/news/nourish-raises-100m-series-c-to-reverse-chronic-disease-with-ai-native-metabolic-clinic-de0abb23-f2e7-4932-a8eb-cd53a53d8e69/); [Similarweb](https://www.similarweb.com/app/google/com.usenourish.app/germany/)
- Fay connects patients to insurance-covered RDs (telehealth sessions) and is AI-powered on the practice side — [Fay/Pulse2](https://pulse2.com/fay-sam-faycurry-profile/)
- Foodvisor's "Coach" plan reportedly paired AI photo logging with RD access, but whether that is current is disputed — [Nutriscan](https://nutriscan.app/blog/posts/foodvisor-vs-lifesum-2026-coaching-plan-vs-self-serve-tracking-a36ad2807b)
- Preppi AI generates calorie-target meal plans with AI and has no human review — [Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/products/preppi-ai/launches)

### Inferences
- The combination of a deterministic gram-exact menu, specialist approval before activation, weekly check-in re-tuning and a plan-aware AI chat appears rare. It is a credible positioning wedge: "a dietitian-approved plan at app price".
- The closest threats are:
  - MFP, if it layers RD review onto Intent plans
  - Nourish, once it ships meal planning on top of its 10,000-RD network
  - Foodvisor in Europe

### Gaps
- MacroFactor's adaptive expenditure algorithm and its meal-plan capabilities in 2026 were not verified with primary sources.
- I did not verify whether Eat This Much or Strongr Fastr added AI chat or weekly auto-adjustment in 2025–26.

## 3. Most common user complaints and unmet needs

### Takeaway
The dominant complaints are monetization (paywalls and upsells, ~29% of negative reviews in one US/UK analysis), database inaccuracy (18%) and ads (15%). Shame-based coaching and cancellation friction also feature, especially for Noom. The open needs are low-friction logging, honest pricing, trustworthy data and plans that feel genuinely personal.

### Cited Findings
- Unstar analyzed 1–3 star reviews of MFP, Lose It!, Noom, Yazio, Lifesum and Cronometer (US/UK, App Store and Google Play; published Apr 23 2026; sample size not stated) — [Unstar](https://unstar.app/blog/calorie-tracking-apps-ranked-1-star-reviews-2026)
  - Complaint categories: paywall/upsell 29%; database errors 18%; ads 15%; shame/psychology tactics 12%; sync with Apple Health/Fitbit 8%; program effectiveness 7%; streak/gamification anxiety 6%; data export/account deletion 5%
  - MFP: barcode scanner moved behind Premium, database quality, ad load
  - Lose It!: premium push, dated UI
  - Cronometer: learning curve, dated UI
  - Lifesum: recipes gated, thin US database, cancellation friction
  - Yazio: surprise auto-renewal, US database gaps, fasting upsell
  - Noom: shame-based coaching, cancellation friction, poor price-to-value
- An April 2026 MFP update (v26.15.0) reportedly introduced bugs that erased meal entries and froze onboarding. Users said logging became "noticeably more effort" — [MWM](https://mwm.ai/articles/myfitnesspal-calorie-counter-v26-15-0-causes-data-loss-and-entry-bugs-april-2026)
- A Reddit post (May 2026) complains that a redesign removed custom foods and quick-log, making macro tracking "almost impossible". This is anecdotal — [Reddit snapshot](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1tqpunc/snapshots/2026-05-29T15%3A30%3A08.235983Z)
- Logging friction and the emotional burden of tracking are cited as the main reasons people abandon calorie apps. This is opinion from vendor blogs — [Rekkon](https://rekkon.pages.dev/blog/why-calorie-counting-fails); [Fitia](https://fitia.app/learn/article/how-to-pick-a-calorie-counter-app-to-stick-with/)
- "Easy to use" is the most frequent praise in recent US App Store reviews (Sept 2026) — [MyNetDiary](https://www.mynetdiary.com/diet-app-scorecard-september-2026.html)

### Inferences
- A prescriptive plan ("what to eat, in grams") lowers logging fatigue compared with open-ended tracking. One-tap swaps and photo checks also fit the "less friction" demand.
- Transparent pricing, easy cancellation and in-app account deletion (which NutriPlan already has) directly address recurring 1-star themes.
- An emotional-eating module built on CBT/DBT, without shame mechanics, counters the "shame/psychology tactics" and "streak anxiety" complaints (18% combined).

### Gaps
- No primary large-sample Reddit analysis was done for r/loseit, r/MacroFactor or r/PCOS, and complaints about "generic meal plans" specifically were not sourced.
- I found no complaint data for MacroFactor, Cal AI or FatSecret.

## 5. Positioning statements and differentiation

### Takeaway
Leaders differentiate by data or AI scale (MFP and Cal AI: database plus photo AI), clinical or medication access (WW Med+, Noom GLP-1Rx: "integrated GLP-1 care"), insurance-covered human experts (Fay, Nourish: RDs covered by insurance) and micronutrient accuracy (Cronometer). ### Inferences
- The white space for NutriPlan:
  1. A non-drug, food-first plan for users who don't want or can't get GLP-1s, or who want a GLP-1-compatible nutrition plan (protein and sarcopenia focus)
  2. Specialist-approved plans without US insurance gating, sold globally at subscription-app price points (~$40–80/yr benchmarks)
  3. Many protocols in one app (menopause, PCOS/insulin resistance, gut/AIP, postpartum), where competitors are single-condition clinics
  4. Lab results driving the plan, like Noom's biomarker add-on at $149/mo but far cheaper
  5. Restaurant-menu photo recommendations as a viral hook beyond plate scanning

### Gaps
- Homepage taglines were not fetched verbatim for MFP, Lose It!, Cronometer, MacroFactor, Yazio, Lifesum, Cal AI, Eat This Much, PlateJoy, Fay or ZOE.
- I found no primary evidence on whether any competitor offers restaurant-menu photo recommendations, blood-test OCR that changes the plan, or supplement-drug interaction checks. This needs targeted verification.
