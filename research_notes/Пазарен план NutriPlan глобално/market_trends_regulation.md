# Global diet/nutrition app and personalized-nutrition market: size, demand trends, trust, and regulatory constraints (as of November 2026)

Research date: 2026-11-07. Every figure is dated. Market-research numbers come from vendor press releases or listings, not the full reports, so methodology wasn't visible. Items marked **[BK]** come from background knowledge and weren't re-verified with a fetched source this session. Treat them as leads to confirm.

## 1. Market size, CAGR, and app-store spend

### Takeaway
"Diet & nutrition apps" is a mid-single-digit-billion USD market. Most 2025 estimates fall between $5B and $6B, with a 12–17% CAGR. One publisher puts it at about $14B. The wider "personalized nutrition" market (supplements, tests, and services as well as apps) is about $14–18B in 2025 and is expected to reach $26–38B by 2030 at roughly 14% CAGR. App-store data shows a mature category: downloads are flat and money per user is rising, with AI features driving the revenue growth.

### Cited Findings

- Per secondary citations of Sensor Tower State of Mobile 2025, the US takes more than 50% of global H&F consumer spend and the UK is second at about 8%. ### Inferences
- For a pitch or plan, use about $5–6B (2025) at about 15% CAGR for "diet & nutrition apps" and about $16B (2025) → about $31B (2030) for "personalized nutrition" (MarketsandMarkets, the most-cited figure). Say that estimates range from $2.4B to $14B.
- The category is mature on downloads and growing on monetization (+13% revenue against +0.8% installs). Growth comes from raising ARPU with premium, AI, and expert tiers, not from cheap reach. That suits a human-dietitian premium tier.
- More than half of category spend is US-based. A "global English" launch is in practice a US+UK-first monetization play, which makes US regulation (FTC, state privacy laws) the main constraint.

## 2. Demand drivers 2025–2026 (GLP-1, AI photo logging, protein, gut, menopause, longevity, UPF, food noise)

### Takeaway
GLP-1 adoption is the biggest structural driver. About 1 in 8 US adults currently uses a GLP-1 (Nov 2025, double May 2024), and incumbents and indie apps launched "GLP-1 companion" products in 2026 built on protein, muscle preservation, and side-effect logging. AI photo logging is the fastest-monetizing feature. Protein (5 straight years as the nutrient Americans most try to get), gut health/fiber, and healthy aging are the strongest consumer food trends. Menopause shows up as a focused women's-health niche.

### Cited Findings
**GLP-1**
- KFF Health Tracking Poll (fielded 27 Oct–2 Nov 2025, n=1,350 US adults): about 12% (1 in 8) currently take a GLP-1 and 18% have ever taken one. In May 2024, 12% had ever taken one and 6% were current users, so current use roughly doubled — [Becker's](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/glp-1s/1-in-8-adults-have-taken-a-glp-1-5-notes/); [KFF May 2024 methodology](https://www.kff.org/report-section/kff-health-tracking-poll-may-2024-the-publics-use-and-views-of-glp-1-drugs-methodology/)
- Current use is 15% among women and 9% among men. The highest rate is in ages 50–64 (22%). 56% of users said the drugs were hard to afford, and the poll was fielded before the presidential announcement on price and coverage — [InsuranceNewsNet/KFF](https://insurancenewsnet.com/oarticle/1-in-8-adults-taking-a-glp-1-drug-for-weight-loss-kff-reports); [Becker's](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/glp-1s/1-in-8-adults-have-taken-a-glp-1-5-notes/)
- MyNetDiary launched a GLP-1 Companion on 5 May 2026 inside Premium ($9.99/mo, $59.99/yr). It includes injection reminders, protein tracking (about 80–120 g/day guidance), GI-symptom logging, and meal planning. It cites the international consensus in Obesity Pillars (Sievenpiper et al., 2025) on prioritizing protein to preserve muscle — [MyNetDiary press release](https://digital-release.kxan.com/business/press-releases/cision/20260505FL48085/mynetdiary-launches-glp-1-companion-for-ozempic-wegovy-and-mounjaro-users)
- Noom GLP-1 Companion added a "Muscle Defense" module with FitOn, combining protein tracking, resistance training, and behavior change — [Noom](https://www.noom.com/health/glp1companion/); [01net](https://www.01net.it/noom-glp-1-companion-to-add-muscle-defense-to-promote-safer-glp-1-use/)
- Indie iOS GLP-1 muscle trackers include Preserve (shot logging, protein, strength; it disclaims "not … medication dosing guidance") and Lean ($79.99/yr, adaptive protein target). Sustain offers camera-based protein estimates and GLP AI focuses on protein and side effects — [Preserve App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id6766536655); [Lean App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6775967571); [Product Hunt Sustain](https://www.producthunt.com/p/sustain); [IndieHackers GLP AI](https://www.indiehackers.com/product/glp-ai)
- Nutritional Outlook (July 2026) argues that generic protein isn't enough and that GLP-1 users need tailored muscle and energy support — [Nutritional Outlook](https://www.nutritionaloutlook.com/view/developing-nutraceutical-companion-solutions-support-long-term-glp-1-user-well-being)
- An IFIC-affiliated piece notes that GLP-1s greatly reduce "food noise" — [IFIC](https://ific.org/?p=21206)

**AI photo logging**
- AI-powered nutrition and diet tracking was among the top H&F apps by IAP revenue growth in 2025. Cal AI is the example of AI keyword-led acquisition — [Sensor Tower](https://sensortower.com/blog/health-and-fitness-apps-ai)

**Protein, gut, healthy aging, diets**
- IFIC 2026 Food & Health Survey: 64% of Americans followed a specific eating pattern or diet in the past year. "Healthy aging" is the top benefit people want from food, and interest in gut, muscle, and brain health goes "beyond weight management" — [Prepared Foods/IFIC](https://www.preparedfoods.com/articles/131965-americans-follow-more-diets-as-health-perceptions-decline)
- Protein has been the nutrient Americans most actively try to consume for 5 consecutive years (IFIC 2025) — [IFIC](https://ific.org/?p=21645)
- Innova Market Insights names gut health and protein as the main consumer drivers for 2026. FoodNavigator's 2026 trend list frames fiber as "the new protein" for satiety and gut health — [Innova](https://www.innovamarketinsights.com/?p=57041); [FoodNavigator](https://foodnavigator.com/Article/2025/12/09/top-10-food-and-beverage-trends-for-2026)
- Green Queen covers a 2026 survey on how protein, UPFs, and GLP-1 are shaping US food habits. I didn't retrieve its UPF figures — [Green Queen](https://www.greenqueen.com.hk/?p=84429)

**Menopause / women's health**
- Tastewise survey: pregnancy/postpartum was the primary health focus, followed by fertility and menopause — [Tastewise](https://tastewise.io/blog/womens-health-trends)

### Inferences
- Ranked by evidence of momentum: (1) GLP-1 companion (protein, muscle, side effects, maintenance after stopping), (2) AI photo logging (monetization proven), (3) protein, then gut/fiber, (4) healthy aging/longevity, (5) women's-health life stages (postpartum, menopause). NutriPlan already has features in each of these areas.
- "GLP-1 companion" is filling up fast (Noom, MyNetDiary, many indie apps). The gap a human dietitian can fill is the *maintenance after stopping* phase and the clinical nuance of exact gram-level menus. Generic protein trackers don't address either.
- Women aged 50–64 combine the highest GLP-1 use with menopause relevance, which makes them a sharp target segment.

- I found no figures on how many people stop GLP-1s or on demand for maintenance after stopping.

## 3. Consumer attitudes: AI nutrition advice vs. human dietitians

### Takeaway
People widely use AI for diet plans but trust in it is split. Many consumers also say they don't want an RD. A human dietitian therefore works better as a trust and safety signal behind the AI ("reviewed by a registered dietitian") than as a pricey core offer.

### Cited Findings
- Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics survey (Jan 2026, n=1,000 US adults): 46% have acted on influencer nutrition advice. One in three have used ChatGPT or another AI to make a nutrition or weight-loss plan, usually without a credentialed expert. 43% don't trust AI for nutrition advice, and 44% aren't interested in working with an RDN/NDTR — [NutritionInsight](https://www.nutritioninsight.com/news/us-consumers-social-media-ai-nutrition-advice-survey.html); [press release](https://business.mammothtimes.com/mammothtimes/article/gnwcq-2026-1-26-new-survey-signals-national-nutrition-crisis-as-misinformation-outpaces-evidence-based-care)
- Purdue CFDAS Consumer Food Insights (Aug 2025): about half report moderate to high trust in AI for food-system roles, especially food safety and nutrition advice. About 15% consistently report no trust — [Purdue](https://ag.purdue.edu/cfdas/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/Report_202508.pdf)
- Ingredient Communications (fielded Oct 2024, n=1,040 UK+US, reported Jan 2025): supplement users trust AI more than non-users. About 59–62% of supplement users would trust AI with diet decisions or genetic-data analysis, and the two reports disagree on the exact figure — [NutritionInsight](https://www.nutritioninsight.com/news/supplement-users-trust-ai-more-than-non-users-survey-reveals.html); [SupplySide SJ](https://www.supplysidesj.com/natural-product-development/supplement-users-more-trusting-of-ai-survey-finds)
- Press coverage (June 2026): dietitians say chatbots can't assess health history or use clinical judgment. A cited study found AI-generated menus averaged about 700 kcal/day less than dietitian menus — [The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2026/06/29/should-you-take-nutrition-advice-from-a-chatbot)
- Sept 2026 coverage: many people remain skeptical of AI in the kitchen — [The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2026/09/27/ai-in-the-kitchenresearch-shows-many-people-remain-sceptical)

### Inferences
- Position NutriPlan as "AI speed, dietitian-checked" and don't sell the dietitian as the main product. The 43% who distrust AI and the 44% who aren't interested in RDs are probably different segments, and one hybrid can address both.
- The finding that AI menus came in about 700 kcal short gives a concrete safety argument for human review of exact-gram menus.

### Gaps
- I found no survey that measures *willingness to pay* extra for human-dietitian review of an AI plan. KFF and Pew AI-in-health polls for 2025–2026 weren't retrieved.
- I have no non-US trust data apart from the UK/US supplement survey.

## 4. Regulation and platform policy: what becomes risky

### Takeaway
In the US, the FDA's January 2026 general-wellness guidance widened room for wellness wearables and apps, but disease-specific claims are still out of bounds: naming a disease, diagnosing, using clinical thresholds, or ongoing disease management. The bigger day-to-day US risks are the FTC (HBNR, deceptive health claims) and state health-privacy laws. In the EU, the binding constraints are GDPR Art. 9 health data, MDR Rule 11 if the software "treats or manages" disease, Reg. 1924/2006 on nutrition and health claims (which hits "detox"), and AI Act transparency. Iridology diagnostics and "detox" claims are the highest-risk parts of the current NutriPlan offer and conflict with credibility positioning.

### Cited Findings
**US — FDA**
- On 6 Jan 2026 the FDA issued final General Wellness and Clinical Decision Support guidances, replacing the 2019 and 2022 versions. They took effect immediately, with no comment period — [King & Spalding](https://www.kslaw.com/insights/articles/fda-updates-general-wellness-and-clinical-decision-support-guidance-documents); [Foley](https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2026/01/digital-health-policy-fda-relaxes-restrictions-over-wearables-and-ai-decision-ma/)
- The wellness guidance now explicitly covers wearables, including non-invasive estimation of BP or glucose. Products still can't name a disease, label outputs as abnormal or diagnostic, use clinical thresholds, give ongoing disease-management alerts, or claim to be "medical/clinical-grade". This followed the July 2025 WHOOP warning letter over its BP Insights feature — [Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-01-20-fda-opens-aperture-for-wearables-in-latest-general-wellness-guidance); [Faegre Drinker](https://faegredrinker.com/en/insights/publications/2026/1/key-updates-in-fdas-2026-general-wellness-and-clinical-decision-support-software-guidance)
- CDS: software that suggests treatment plans gets enforcement discretion when a *healthcare provider* reviews, revises, and finalizes the plan. Developers must give clinicians clear documentation — [Foley](https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2026/01/digital-health-policy-fda-relaxes-restrictions-over-wearables-and-ai-decision-ma/)
- **[BK]** The 2019 General Wellness guidance (carried into 2026) allows wellness claims tied to chronic diseases only where healthy lifestyle is *well understood* to reduce risk or help people live well with the condition, e.g. "may help reduce the risk of type 2 diabetes". It doesn't allow claims to treat or manage the disease. Confirm in the FDA text.

**US — FTC and privacy**
- Health Breach Notification Rule amendments (announced 26 Apr 2024, effective 29 Jul 2024, 3–2 vote) explicitly cover health apps and similar technologies. "Breach" now includes *unauthorized disclosure*, such as sharing with ad or analytics SDKs without authorization, not just hacks. Civil penalties apply for failing to notify — [Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/es/insights/2024/07/ftc-issues-final-health-breach-notification-rule); [McDermott](https://www.mwe.com/?p=279089); [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/node/78112)
- Washington My Health My Data Act: "consumer health data" is defined broadly and excludes HIPAA PHI. It covers WA residents and data processed in WA, has a private right of action (damages must be proven), requires stricter consent for sale, and limits geofencing — [Privacy & Security Academy](https://www.privacysecurityacademy.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/2023-Fall-PS-Forum-health-privacy-and-security-workshop-1.pdf)
- **[BK]** MHMDA has applied since 31 Mar 2024 (small businesses from 30 Jun 2024). Similar laws exist in Nevada (SB 370) and Connecticut (consumer health data amendments to its privacy act). The FTC's HBNR actions against GoodRx (Feb 2023) and Premom/Easy Healthcare (May 2023) set the precedent for penalizing pixel/SDK sharing. **[BK]** HIPAA generally doesn't apply to a direct-to-consumer app unless it works for or on behalf of a covered entity, such as a clinic or insurer. Confirm.

**EU**
- AI Act: Article 5 prohibitions have applied since Feb 2025 and GPAI rules since Aug 2025. Art. 50 transparency duties (telling users they're dealing with AI, labeling AI content) apply from Aug 2026. The Digital Omnibus provisional agreement (7 May 2026; Parliament vote 16 Jun 2026) moves Annex III high-risk obligations to 2 Dec 2027 and Annex I (product-embedded, e.g. medical devices under MDR) to 2 Aug 2028. Watermarking for systems already on the market moves to 2 Dec 2026. I found no confirmation of publication in the Official Journal, so check — [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines/); [Sidley](https://datamatters.sidley.com/2026/06/22/eu-lawmakers-reach-provisional-agreement-to-delay-key-eu-ai-act-obligations/); [CMS](https://cms.law/en/gbr/publication/The-EU-s-Digital-Omnibus-on-AI-What-the-Provisional-Agreement-Really-Changes)
- **[BK]** A wellness nutrition AI isn't high-risk under the AI Act unless it's a safety component of, or itself, an MDR medical device that requires notified-body assessment (Art. 6(1)). Then it's high-risk with the Aug 2028 deadline (after the Omnibus). MDR Rule 11: software that provides information used for diagnostic or therapeutic decisions is class IIa or higher, so "clinical protocols" for disease management could pull NutriPlan into MDR. GDPR Art. 9: health data needs explicit consent (or another Art. 9 basis), a DPIA is likely needed, and transfer rules apply.
- Google Play's EU change (effective 28 Jan 2026) aligns the Health & Medical Functionalities policy with MDCG guidance for medical-device apps aimed at EU users — [Google Play Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16679511?hl=en)
- EU/UK claims rules: the ASA upheld complaints that "Detox" (Pukka Detox tea, 2018) is a health claim under Reg. 1924/2006 and needs an accompanying *authorised* claim. ASA guidance says general detox claims for food are likely health claims. A March 2026 law-firm article calls "detox" one of the most misused wellness terms — [ASA Detoxing guidance](https://www.asa.org.uk/Advice-Training-on-the-rules/Advice-Online-Database/Detoxing-General.aspx); [Natural Products Online](https://naturalproductsonline.co.uk/?p=35489); [Mishcon de Reya](https://www.mishcon.com/news/weight-loss-and-detoxing-claims-in-advertising-what-brand-owners-need-to-know)

**UK — iridology**
- ASA: any claim that iridology detects or diagnoses current or future health issues is likely to breach the code unless backed by robust evidence such as human clinical trials. It has ruled against iridology diagnostic claims (Sarah Burt ruling; live-blood-test ruling covering allergies, eczema, fatigue, and hormonal imbalance) — [ASA iridology](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/health-iridology.html); [ASA Sarah Burt ruling](https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/sarah-burt-a15-307899.html); [ASA live blood test](https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/live-blood-test-a12-214202.html)

**App stores**
- Google Play: health apps must complete the Health apps declaration in Play Console and publish a privacy policy covering sensitive data. Regulated medical devices must declare it and get a "Medical Device" label. Other apps must state they are "not a medical device and does not diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any medical condition". Health Connect access goes through the Play Console declaration, and sensitive health-record safeguards were tightened on 5 Mar 2025 — [Google Play Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16679511?hl=en); [Android Developers – publish health app](https://developer.android.com/health-and-fitness/health-connect/publish); [Policy announcement 5 Mar 2025](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/15931464)
- Apple Guideline 1.4.1: medical apps that could give inaccurate data or be used to diagnose or treat get extra scrutiny. Accuracy claims need disclosed methodology. Developers report rejections for "diagnoses or treatment advice without appropriate regulatory clearance" and for health recommendations *without citing sources*, including a weight-loss tips app. Apps that call themselves wellness-only still get flagged — [Apple Dev Forums 696430](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/696430); [807508](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/807508); [772732](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/772732); [ConductAtlas summary](https://conductatlas.com/platform/apple/apple-app-store-review-guidelines/medical-app-accuracy-and-regulatory-clearance-requirements/)

### Inferences: feature risk map for NutriPlan
- **Highest risk, remove or rebrand before a global launch:** *AIRIS iris diagnostics* (ASA treats iridology diagnosis as unsubstantiated, and it's likely to fail Apple 1.4.1 "diagnosis without clearance" and the FTC substantiation standard; it also damages the credibility of the rest of the product) and the *"detox" protocol* (a health claim under EU/UK law without an authorised claim, and an FTC substantiation risk in the US). Rename to something like "reset / whole-food week" with no toxin language.
- **High risk depending on wording:** the "11 clinical protocols" for insulin resistance, autoimmune, GI, and similar. The word "clinical" plus a named disease plus adaptive management looks like "treat/manage disease", which falls outside FDA wellness, is likely MDR Rule 11 class IIa in the EU, and invites Apple 1.4.1 rejection. Lower-risk framing: "nutrition programs for people with / supporting …" with lifestyle-level claims; plans created and signed off by a registered dietitian; a requirement that the user has a diagnosis and clinician from outside the app; no lab-threshold interpretation that leads to treatment decisions. Under the FDA CDS route, enforcement discretion needs a *healthcare provider* to finalize the plan. That suits the human-dietitian model only if the RD is properly credentialed and the AI output is presented as support for the RD.
- **Medium risk:** blood-test upload and interpretation (flagging values as abnormal or applying clinical thresholds goes past the FDA wellness line); wearable and CGM insights (no disease alerts); *supplement recommendations and supplement–drug interaction advice* (drug-interaction guidance is a clinical function, and GLP-1 dosing advice should be avoided entirely, as Preserve's disclaimer does); emotional eating (don't diagnose eating disorders; screen and refer).
- **Privacy is a big practical risk:** any analytics or ad SDK that receives health-related events can count as an HBNR "breach" or an MHMDA violation, and EU users need explicit Art. 9 consent. AI chat and photo data sent to LLM providers need a DPA and a transfer basis.
- **Credibility signals that also reduce risk:** named credentialed dietitians (RD/RDN in the US, HCPC-registered dietitian in the UK); "reviewed by an RD" on every plan; inline citations, which Apple explicitly checks; a clinical advisory board; clear "not a medical device" disclaimers; and AI-disclosure labels (EU AI Act Art. 50 from Aug 2026).
